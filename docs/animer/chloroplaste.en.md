# Phase 2 – Diving into the chloroplast

This phase relies entirely on the 3D-printed pieces. It is **the most
hands-on** part of the workshop: every participant is invited to come and
have a go!

**You will need:** the chloroplast, the thylakoids, the photosynthetic chain,
the Calvin cycle box, **marbles and sweets**
([see the kit](../materiel.md)).

## How it goes

1. **The chloroplast** opens to reveal the stacked **thylakoids**: the real
   site of photosynthesis.
2. We zoom in on a piece of membrane, covered with a myriad of small
   complexes… some of which are green.
3. Final zoom: **the photosynthetic chain**, with its complexes lined up on
   the membrane in the order of the reaction, together with the **Calvin
   cycle** box, which makes the sugars.

## The sequence to explain at least once

<div class="grid" markdown>

!!! note "1. Light splits water"
    At **photosystem II**, light energy pulls electrons away from water.
    Oxygen is released (this is the O~2~ we breathe) and H^+^ ions are left
    behind.

!!! note "2. The electrons travel"
    The electrons (**the marbles**!) move from complex to complex along the
    chute, to **photosystem I**, then on to the Calvin cycle.

!!! note "3. The H^+^ ions turn the turbine"
    As they cross back through the membrane via **ATP synthase**, the H^+^
    ions spin its "head" like a mill wheel: it makes energy (ATP) for the
    Calvin cycle.

!!! note "4. Sugar is made"
    Using the electrons and the ATP, the enzymes of the **Calvin cycle**
    capture CO~2~ from the air and produce sugar (**the sweets**!).

</div>

Then let participants run the chain as they like. Remember: **no
electrons… no sweets!**

!!! tip "From experience"
    From around age 9, pupils already understand electron transport quite
    well – and they love this part!

## The version for young children

![](../images/niveaux/niveau-1.png){ width="50" align=left }

For the youngest (ages 6–7), the details of the chain are too complex.
Replace this phase with the **simplified model**: a large leaf that receives
water, CO~2~ and sunlight, and produces sugar and oxygen.

<div class="equation">water + CO<sub>2</sub> + light → sugar + O<sub>2</sub></div>

The large water, CO~2~, sugar and O~2~ pieces act as reservoirs and are easy
for children to handle.
