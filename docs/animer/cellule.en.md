# Phase 1 – Discovering the plant cell

We start from what everyone knows, **the plant**, then go into **the leaf**,
and then into **the cell**.

**You will need:** the leaf box, the cell model and, if possible, a
microscope ([see the kit](../materiel.md)).

## Looking through the microscope (optional)

Ideally, this phase is combined with a look through the microscope. But
don't worry if you don't have one: the 3D pieces are enough to dive into the
cell!

??? example "Preparing the sample"
    - If you work in a plant biology laboratory, young seedlings (1 to 2
      weeks after germination) on a Petri dish are easy to carry around.
    - Otherwise, any leaf, blade of grass or piece of moss will do, as long
      as it is bright green and thin.
    - Place a thin piece of leaf in a drop of water, between a slide and a
      coverslip. If it is too thick, gently press on the coverslip with the
      back of your fingernail.

Participants then discover a transparent cell, dotted with green spots:
**the chloroplasts**! By moving around the sample, they can also observe
**the stomata**.

## Explaining with the 3D models

Accompany the observation with the **leaf box**, whose panels describe the
structures of the leaf, and then with the **cell model**, from which each
compartment can be taken out.

=== "In the classroom"

    The leaf and the cell can be presented to the whole class, while also
    projecting the leaf box visuals. If several microscopes are available
    (schools sometimes have some), divide the pupils into parallel groups,
    with worksheets to record their observations.

=== "General public"

    With smaller groups, the microscope observation and the explanations
    with the 3D models can take place side by side.

=== "Young children"

    Choose the **cell "as seen under the microscope"**: a huge vacuole that
    pushes the chloroplasts against the edge, exactly what you see through
    the eyepiece.

## Key ideas

<!-- Summary; the full explanations are in the guide. -->

- Plants, like us, are made of **cells**, the "building blocks" of living
  things, too small to be seen with the naked eye.
- The plant cell is surrounded by a rigid **cell wall**, a feature specific
  to plants.
- Inside: the **nucleus** (the DNA), the **mitochondria** (respiration), a
  large water-filled **vacuole** that keeps the cell firm… and the
  **chloroplasts**, which give the leaf its green colour.
- On the underside of the leaf, the **stomata** are tiny mouths that let
  gases and water in and out.
